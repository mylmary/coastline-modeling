# Coastline-modeling
Solving Partial Differential Equations/Wave propagation near the shore

 
The region combines extreme bathymetric contrasts—where 500-meter-deep ocean trenches plunge directly into shallow 5-meter island shelves—with dual-forcing mechanisms from seasonal monsoon wind stress and the persistent, northward-flowing East African Coastal Current. 

Simulating how these forces interact with highly irregular geometries, like the jagged barrier reefs of Lamu or the sprawling river networks of the Rufiji Delta, creates a punishing testing ground.

Standard grid-based solvers require millions of cells and slow down calculations to resolve these sharp discontinuities, making this coastline the ideal dataset to prove the speed and accuracy of next-generation neural operators like NVIDIA Transolver.


Furthermore, mastering simulations for this specific region delivers critical, predictive data for climate resilience and coastal infrastructure management. 

Accurately modeling wave shoaling, tidal variations, and sediment transport allows researchers to simulate long-term coastal erosion and storm surge risks threatening low-lying communities and dense blue carbon sinks. 

It also enables engineers to run predictive hydrodynamic simulations around major East African shipping hubs like Mombasa and Dar es Salaam. 
By transitioning from slow, traditional physics engines to Transolver's real-time slice-attention over unstructured point clouds, scientists can instantly simulate multiple climate change scenarios—such as rising sea levels or intensified monsoon storms—to design targeted, data-driven coastal protection strategies.
